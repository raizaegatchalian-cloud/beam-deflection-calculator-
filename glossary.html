Guide to Symbols, Diagrams & Terms

New to beam analysis or unfamiliar with some of the words and drawings in the calculator? Here is a simple, plain-English guide to every shape, arrow, chart, and engineering term you will see on the page.

How to Use BeamStudio 3D (Step-by-Step Process)

Follow these 4 simple steps to set up any beam, view it in 3D, and read its full step-by-step solutions.

Step 1 · Start Here & Beam Setup Left Panel

Either pick a Ready-Made Example at the top of the left panel to auto-fill everything, or type your own Length (m), pick a Material (or check Treat EI as Constant), and select a Support Condition.

Optional: Open Cross-Section & Stiffness (E, I) to pick a 3D beam shape (I-Beam, Rect, Tube, T-Beam) or enter a custom I in m⁴, cm⁴, mm⁴, or in⁴.
Step 2 · Add Applied Loads Forces & Moments

Choose a load type (Point, UDL, UVL, or Moment), enter its magnitude and position(s) along the beam, and click + Add Load to Beam. You can combine multiple loads on the same beam.

Sign rule: Positive vertical loads push downward (↓); positive moments twist counterclockwise (↺).
Step 3 · Pick Method & Calculate 3 Methods

Choose Double Integration, Moment-Area, Conjugate Beam, or Compare All 3, then click Calculate Beam. Your 3D beam, summary numbers, diagrams, and equations update instantly.

Unit switch: Use the mm | cm | m toggle on the Max Deflection card anytime to convert units in 1 click.
Step 4 · Inspect Any Point & Read Solutions Explore

Drag the 3D beam to rotate it, slide Inspect Point x (or hover any chart) to check any spot along the span, and switch tabs below between Diagrams, Max Deflection Solution, Point x Solution, and AI Solver & Tutor.

Two solutions: Check Max Deflection Solution for peak sag, or Point x Solution for the exact step-by-step slope & deflection at your chosen x!

1. Support Symbols (What Holds the Beam Up)

These shapes appear beneath the 3D beam and in the 2D setup diagram to show how the beam is anchored.

Pin Support Triangle

Acts like a door hinge. It stops the beam from moving up, down, or sideways, but still lets the end tilt freely as the beam bends.

Result: Vertical deflection is 0 here, but slope (tilt) is allowed.
Roller Support Triangle + Wheels

Rests on rollers so the beam cannot sink downward, while allowing it to tilt and slide slightly sideways if it expands or contracts.

Result: Vertical deflection is 0, and it pushes straight up (or down).
Fixed Support Anchored Wall

Tightly clamped into a rigid wall (like a diving board or balcony). It stops both movement and tilting completely.

Result: Both deflection and slope are 0 right at the wall.
Internal Hinge Circle Joint

Appears inside the Mirror (Conjugate) Beam whenever the real beam has an interior support (like an overhanging beam).

Meaning: Mirror moment is 0 here — matching real deflection = 0 at that support.

2. Load Arrows & Forces (What Pushes on the Beam)

These rose/violet arrows show external weights or twisting forces pushing on your beam.

Point Load P (kN)

A single concentrated weight pressing at one exact spot on the beam (like a heavy column or person standing at one point).

Sign: Positive (+) pushes downward ↓. Negative (−) pulls upward ↑.
Uniform Load (UDL) w (kN/m)

Evenly spread weight across a stretch of the beam (like a brick wall, concrete slab, or snow resting uniformly from a to b).

Total weight: Intensity w multiplied by loaded length (b − a).
Varying Load (UVL) w₁ → w₂

A spread load that ramps smoothly from one intensity (w₁) to another (w₂), forming a triangle or trapezoid (like water or soil pressure).

Tip: Set w₁ = 0 for a triangle growing to the right, or w₂ = 0 to taper down.
Applied Moment M₀ (kN·m)

A pure twisting effort applied at a point on the beam (like turning a wrench bolted onto the beam).

Sign: Positive (+) twists counterclockwise ↺. Negative (−) twists clockwise ↻.

3. Understanding the 3D Beam & Diagrams

What the visual overlays, colors, and charts on the calculator actually show.

Ghost Outline & Bend Scale 3D Stage

The faint wireframe shows where the beam starts when flat. Real steel beams bend only a few millimeters, so Bend Scale exaggerates the bend visually so you can clearly see the curve.

Tip: Keep Bend Scale around 1.0× (setting it to 0.0× hides the visual curve).
3D Color Map Heatmap

Colors the 3D beam by intensity. For Bending Moment, Aurora Blue marks sagging ("smiling") zones while Mauve/Wisteria marks hogging ("frowning") zones.

Tip: Switch to Clean Opaline if you prefer a neutral metallic look.
Shear Force Chart (SFD) V(x)

Shows the internal up-and-down slicing force at every point along the beam. Sudden vertical jumps happen right where a Point Load or Support acts.

Shortcut: Where Shear crosses zero is usually where Bending Moment peaks!
Bending Moment Chart (BMD) M(x)

Shows how hard the beam is trying to bend at every spot. The tallest peak is the most stressed location along the span.

Sign: Above zero = sagging ("smile"). Below zero = hogging ("frown").
Bending Areas & Balance Points M/EI & x̄

Used in the Moment-Area method. It splits the bending curve into clean shaded shapes (A₁, A₂…) and marks each shape's balance point (centroid dot x̄).

Why it matters: Shaded areas give change in tilt; areas × distance give bend!
Bent Beam vs. Reference Line Deviation t

Draws a straight dashed tangent line touching the bent beam at one reference support. The vertical gap t between that straight line and the curve is called the tangential deviation.

Shortcut: Dividing that gap t by the span gives the support's starting tilt!

4. Key Engineering Words & Units

Plain-English definitions of every number and symbol shown in the sidebar and results bar.

Deflection δ or y (mm, cm, m)

How far a point on the beam moves vertically from its original straight line under load. Negative (−) means downward sag; positive (+) means upward lift.

Slope (Tilt) θ (rad / mrad)

The tilt angle of the bent beam at any spot. 1 mrad = 0.001 radians (about 0.057 degrees). Where slope equals 0 is usually where the beam sags the deepest.

Support Reactions R_A, R_B, M_A

The invisible upward/downward push-back forces (and wall twisting moments) that the supports supply to keep the beam standing still in balance.

Young’s Modulus E (GPa)

Measures how stiff the material is. Steel (~200 GPa) bends much less than Aluminum (~69 GPa) or Timber (~11 GPa) of the exact same size.

Moment of Inertia I (m⁴, cm⁴, mm⁴)

Measures how stiff the cross-section shape is against bending. A ruler laid flat bends easily (small I), while the same ruler turned on its edge is very stiff (large I).

Unit tip: 1 m⁴ = 10⁸ cm⁴ = 10¹² mm⁴. Always match the unit dropdown to your problem!
Flexural Rigidity & Constant EI EI (kN·m²)

Combined bending stiffness (E × I). If a homework problem doesn't give numbers for E and I and says "assume EI is constant", check Treat EI as Constant to see answers cleanly in terms of /EI.

Span Ratio L / δ

Compares beam length to how much it sags (e.g., L/360 means the sag is 1/360th of the beam's length). Building codes use limits like L/360 or L/240 so floors don't feel bouncy.

Centroid x̄ (m)

The exact horizontal balance point (center of area) of a shaded shape on the bending diagram, measured from the left end of the beam.

5. The 3 Calculation Methods Explained Simply

All three methods give the exact same final deflection — they just take different routes to get there.

1. Double Integration Calculus Route

Writes a formula for the Bending Moment M(x), integrates it once to get beam tilt (slope), integrates a second time to get beam bend (deflection), and uses the support positions to find the exact numbers.

Best for: Getting exact formulas valid at every point along the beam.
2. Moment-Area Method Geometry Route

Uses the geometry of the M/EI diagram instead of heavy calculus: the shaded area between two points gives the change in tilt, and the area multiplied by its balance distance gives the bend offset.

Best for: Quickly finding slope or deflection at a single target spot.
3. Conjugate Beam Method Mirror-Beam Route

Builds an imaginary "mirror" beam of the same length with swapped supports (Fixed ↔ Free, Interior Support ↔ Hinge) and loads it with the M/EI diagram. Solving normal shear and moment on this mirror beam directly gives real slope and real deflection!

Best for: Overhanging beams and beams with changing stiffness.
